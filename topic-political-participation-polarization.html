Political Sociology Topic

Political Participation and Polarization

Research on political participation, affective polarization, ideological polarization, political trust, democratic engagement, political news consumption, and comparative political behaviour in Slovenia, Central and Eastern Europe, and wider European contexts.

Overview

Comparative Political Behaviour Research

This topic brings together research on political participation, polarization, political information, trust, democratic engagement, and civic engagement.

Recent work investigates the relationship between affective polarization and political participation, the role of ideological polarization in the relationship between political news consumption and participation across 24 countries, and longitudinal relationships between perceived ideological polarization, institutional trust, and vaccination attitudes.

A dedicated comparative project examined political participation in the age of polarization across Austria, Poland, and Slovenia.
